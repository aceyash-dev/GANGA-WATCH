Treatment capacity quadruples since 2017, yet faecal coliform at Malviya Bridge still runs double the bathing limit

Sewage treatment in Varanasi has grown from 100 MLD to 420 MLD in seven years. Dissolved oxygen now sits comfortably above the CPCB threshold at every monitored station. The remaining gap is bacterial.

Between 2017 and 2024, the stretch of the Ganga passing through Varanasi moved from CPCB Priority Class IV to Priority Class V — a modest but measurable improvement in the river's organic load. The shift reflects a near-quadrupling of municipal sewage treatment capacity, from roughly 100 million litres per day to 420 MLD across four new and upgraded plants.

Dissolved oxygen, the clearest single indicator of a river's capacity to support aquatic life, has risen from a median of 6.8 mg/L in 2017 to 8.0 mg/L in 2024. Every monitored station now reports above the CPCB outdoor bathing requirement of 5 mg/L.

Biochemical oxygen demand tells a similar story, falling from 5.2 mg/L to 3.2 mg/L over the same period. It remains marginally above the ≤ 3 mg/L bathing criterion, and the downstream station at Malviya Bridge reports 4.0 mg/L — the highest in the monitored set.

The persistent outlier is faecal coliform. Upstream readings have fallen sharply, from around 1,300 MPN/100 mL in 2017 to 490 in 2024. Downstream of the city, however, the figure stands at 4,900 MPN/100 mL — just under double the 2,500 MPN/100 mL bathing threshold, and roughly ten times the upstream value.

The upstream–downstream differential is the clearest signal in the dataset. It points to continued discharge between the two stations, most of it from unsewered wards and drain outfalls that the treatment network has not yet reached, rather than from the plants themselves.

On the live figures. River discharge and rainfall in the ticker and third chart are fetched at page load from Open-Meteo, which requires no API key and publishes modelled flow from the GloFAS river network alongside gridded precipitation. Coordinates used are 25.3176° N, 82.9739° E — the mid-point of the Varanasi stretch.

Station chemistry (DO, BOD, coliform, pH) is not available from any keyless public endpoint. The CPCB real-time water quality network at rtwqmsdb1.cpcb.gov.in and the NMCG monitoring portal both sit behind institutional access. The values shown here are the published December 2024 medians and should be read as a snapshot, not a live reading.

Discharge is a useful companion to the chemistry: high flow dilutes coliform loading, low flow concentrates it. Where the two datasets overlap in time, coliform counts at Malviya Bridge track the seasonal discharge curve inversely — highest in the pre-monsoon lean season, lowest during the monsoon peak.
